Exercise 8
==========

The photo of Fig. 4.6 was taken at an angle and the page wasn't flat, so the
plot frame came out as a slightly curved trapezoid. Reading coordinates straight
off the photo would have been off by a lot near the corners.

While looking at the tick marks I noticed the x axis is not linear in
wavelength. 400->500 nm is about 645 pixels but 500->600 nm is only about 430,
ratio 1.5, which is exactly (1/400-1/500)/(1/500-1/600). So the axis is linear
in 1/lambda (wavenumber) and just labeled in nm. Fitting the tick positions
linear in 1/lambda gave 1.6 px rms, linear in lambda gave 157 px.

rectify_figure.py finds the frame and all the tick marks on the four edges
(370-680 nm every 10 nm, absorbance 0-100 every 10), fits 1/lambda and
absorbance as cubic polynomials of pixel position, and resamples the photo into
fig_4_6_rectified.png where x is linear in wavelength (0.25 nm/px) and y is
linear in absorbance (0.1/px). Residual at the ticks was about 0.6 nm and 0.08
in absorbance. As a check that wasn't part of the fit, the lines at 420, 498,
534 and 564 nm go right through the peak arrows printed on the figure.

Then I clicked the markers with digitize.py, one curve at a time:
blue 17 points, rods 21, green 26, red 28. Almost every point landed within
about 1 nm of a multiple of 10 nm, which is how the original data was sampled.
Around 400-420 nm the rod, green and red markers sit on top of each other and
at 410 nm I couldn't tell which marker belongs to the red curve, so red has no
410 point and the spline fills that gap.

For part b, Photoreceptor reads a curve file and interpolates it with a cubic
spline (Qat InterpolatingFunction). Past the last measured point the spline
extrapolates linearly, which would go negative, so I clip the response at 0.
The four curves are plotted together in photoreceptor_response.png. Evaluated at
the published peaks the response comes out 100.0 (blue, 420), 100.4 (rods,
498), 100.9 (green, 534) and 100.4 (red, 564), which is what you'd expect for
normalized absorbance.
