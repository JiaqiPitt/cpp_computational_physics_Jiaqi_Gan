Holy Dog Teacher and the Double Pendulum

I started out wanting a double pendulum and nothing else, written with SoQt/Coin like the Chapter 10 examples. 
The rods are aluminium and have mass, and each one ends in a bone biscuit that turns with its rod, so the biscuit's own moment of inertia also goes into the equations. 
From the Lagrangian, the two equations of motion become a 2x2 linear system for the angular accelerations, which I solve at every RK4 step. 
The time step is 1/1200 s, 20 steps per 1/60 s animation frame. Checks: with massless rods it matches the textbook formula to 1e-14, the energy (about 0.1 J) drifts by less than 1e-7 J in a minute, 
and the small-oscillation periods (0.987 s and 0.417 s) agree with the normal modes from the eigenvalue problem.

A pendulum on a white screen got boring fast, so I put it on a teacher's desk, and then the desk needed a teacher. I had an AI(ChatGPT Image 2.5 model) turn my dog BuBu into "dog Jesus" (halo, robe, open arms)
and made him into a plywood cutout on a stick standing behind the desk. The outline comes from a mask of the photo, traced with marching squares and simplified to 174 points. The halo is a separate ring behind the head;
I fitted a circle to it in the photo (radius 263.6 px, rms 2.3 px). There is a torsion spring at the bottom of the stick, so the cutout is an inverted pendulum with a spring: I phi'' = -k phi + M g h sin(phi). 
I and M h come from the area moments of the outline polygon (Green's theorem) plus the ring and the stick, giving about 4.2 kg and I = 4.6 kg m^2, and k is set for a 1.6 s sway.

Then came the extras: a Pitt plaque on the front of the desk (from the official brand files), an open book next to him, and a speech bubble drawn with QPainter on a card that always faces the camera. 
He preaches "The Sermon on the Pendulum", ten lines shown one after another, each a Bible verse bent into physics: 
"In the beginning was the Lagrangian", 
"Let him who is without sin θ cast the first biscuit", 
"I can walk on water. I cannot predict this pendulum", and finally "Peace be with you. Homework is due Friday."


The hardest part was the lighting, not the physics. With SoShadowGroup, the floor in my first test renders came out black and the front panel of the desk disappeared, 
because the textures were not loaded yet in the first frame. The dog looked much darker than the photo, so the print got a little emissive light. 
His large outline polygon smeared the desk's shadow across his robe, so I had to change his shadow settings. The book grew grey stripes by shadowing itself; 
setting it to "cast shadows only" did not help, and it needed no shadows at all. Every fix was small, but each one took a while to find.
