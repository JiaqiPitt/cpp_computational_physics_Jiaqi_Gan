Exercise 7.8 notes

Method

Atomic units throughout. The protons are fixed at +-z d/2, so there is only the electron's kinetic term (the book writes two, but there is one electron).

I didn't need the Laplacian. Each phi_i is the exact ground state of its own proton, so H phi1 = (-1/2 - 1/r2) phi1 and H phi2 = (-1/2 - 1/r1) phi2. That makes H psi = -psi/2 - phi1/r2 -+ phi2/r1, which only needs the two distances.

For the Monte Carlo I sampled points from p = (phi1^2 + phi2^2)/2 instead of a uniform box: pick one proton at random, draw r from a gamma distribution (shape 3, scale 1/2, i.e. density 4 r^2 exp(-2r)), and pick a uniform direction. I used 200000 points for each d, with the same seed for every d so the curves come out smooth. The exact LCAO formulas are printed next to the MC numbers as a check.

b) Energy vs d

The mean of psi H psi/p estimates <psi|H|psi>, and E = <psi|H|psi>/<psi|psi> + 1/d (the 1/d is the proton-proton repulsion).
Exact: E = -1/2 + 1/d - (J +- K)/(1 +- S), with J = 1/d - exp(-2d)(1 + 1/d) and K = exp(-d)(1 + d).

Do you predict a bound system?

Yes, in the symmetric state. E_s drops below -0.5 hartree (an H atom with the proton far away) and has a minimum near d = 2.5 Bohr radii (about 1.3 Angstrom), 1.76 eV below -0.5. The antisymmetric state is always above -0.5 and keeps falling as d grows, so it doesn't bind. The real H2+ is bound by 2.79 eV at d = 2.0. This trial function gives less binding, which it has to, since a variational energy can't go below the true one.
