Exercise 7
==========

This is a 1D step potential scattering problem. Region I (x<0) is free,
region II (x>=0) has height V. Set hbar=1, 2m=1 so k1=sqrt(E) and
k2=sqrt(E-V), using std::complex for k2 so it automatically comes out real
when E>V and purely imaginary when E<V - never had to write an if/else for
the two energy regimes, which was the whole point of the exercise. Matched
psi and psi' at x=0 and solved the usual two equations for B and C:

B = (k1-k2)/(k1+k2)
C = 2*k1/(k1+k2)
R = |B|^2
T = (Re(k2)/k1)*|C|^2

I ran it for E=1 fixed and V/E from 0 up to 3. Data (from barrier, R+T column
added by the program itself as a sanity check):

R+T=1 every single row, so conservation checks out and I'm fairly confident
the complex algebra is right.

The part that surprised me is V/E=1 giving R=1, T=0 exactly, not
some smooth number in between. At E=V, k2=0, so the "transmitted" wave in
region II is just a flat constant C, not travelling anywhere, so it carries
zero probability current even though it has a nonzero amplitude sitting
there. I added a few extra points close to the threshold (0.9, 0.95, 0.99)
just to check this wasn't some discontinuity/bug - it's not, R climbs
continuously and pretty steeply from 0.11 at 0.75 up to 1.0 right at V=E,
it's just a sharp rise, not a jump. Apparently this is a known thing called
threshold reflection - even though classically a particle with E>V always
gets through, quantum mechanically the reflection probability goes all the
way to 1 as you approach E=V from above.

Also I tested the actual wavefunction for two cases (V/E=0.5 and V/E=2.0),
step size 0.05 from x=-10 to 10, written straight to CSV files from the
program using ofstream (wavefunction_above.csv, wavefunction_below.csv),
then plotted with plot_wavefunctions.py -> wavefunctions.png. Things that
match what they should:

- For V/E=0.5 (E>V), |psi|^2 oscillates in region I (interference between
  incident and reflected wave) but is a flat constant in region II - makes
  sense, only one travelling wave there, nothing to interfere with itself.
  Checked the flat value against T*k1/Re(k2): comes out to 1.3726, matches
  the CSV exactly.
- For V/E=2.0 (E<V), region II decays like exp(-2*kappa*x) with
  kappa=sqrt(V-E)=1. Checked: |psi(1)|^2/|psi(0)|^2 = 0.2707/2.0 = 0.1353,
  and exp(-2) = 0.1353. Matches.
- psi and its value at x=0 line up between the two region formulas in both
  cases, so the boundary matching in the code is doing what it's supposed to.

Added a V(x) step and a horizontal E line to the plot so you can actually
see whether the energy line sits above or below the barrier top, which
makes the E>V vs E<V distinction obvious at a glance instead of just
inferring it from V/E.
