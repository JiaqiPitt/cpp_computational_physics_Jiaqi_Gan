Exercise 2
==========

I computed the relativistic stretch-factor gamma two different ways and
compared them as beta gets closer and closer to 1. The direct formula is
gamma = 1/sqrt(1 - beta^2), and the "stable" formula rewrites 1 - beta^2 as
(1-beta)(1+beta) = eps*(2-eps), where eps = 1 - beta. I fed in
eps = 1e-1, 1e-2, ..., down to 1e-16 and used the stable double-precision
result as the reference to measure the fractional error of the direct
method against. Full output of the program is saved in gamma_table.txt,
here's the actual data (copy-pasted straight from the terminal):

(beta prints as exactly 1.0000000000e+00 from row 12 onward - that's just
double running out of bits to represent "1 minus something tiny" as
distinct from 1. gamma_direct still keeps changing after that point though,
because 1.0 - beta*beta isn't necessarily exactly zero even when beta itself
displays as 1.0, it depends on the last few bits that don't show up when
printed with only 10 digits)

With double, the direct formula actually holds up a lot longer than I
expected. The fractional error stays under 1e-3 all the way down to
eps = 1e-15, and only blows up at eps = 1e-16 (error jumps to about 5%).
So the max beta you can use the naive formula for, if you need 0.1%
accuracy, is roughly beta = 1 - 1e-15 in double precision.

Then I added a float version of the direct formula just to see how much
worse it is, and it's pretty dramatic. The error crosses the 1e-3 line
between eps = 1e-5 (still fine, 6.8e-4) and eps = 1e-6 (blows past it,
6.6e-3). So with float you only get to beta ~ 1 - 1e-5 before you lose the
required accuracy - about ten orders of magnitude earlier than double.
And once eps gets below float's machine epsilon (~1.19e-7, this is the same
number from exercise 1), 1 - eps just rounds to exactly 1.0f, so
1 - beta_f*beta_f becomes exactly 0 and the whole thing turns into inf.
That's not really "losing accuracy" anymore, it's losing all the
significant digits at once.

Basically this is the same catastrophic cancellation problem from Taylor
series work in Chapter 1 - subtracting two nearly-equal floating point
numbers (1 and beta^2) wipes out the digits that actually mattered. The
stable formula never subtracts two close numbers like that (eps is given
directly, not computed by subtraction), so gamma_stable never showed any
blow-up in the table, not even at eps = 1e-16.
